## Process

1. On 2026-08-31, begin the batch with approximately 1 L of fresh apple juice made from apples from the yard.
2. Prepare approximately 1 L of tea using black tea, mint, and Whittard Plum & Cinnamon instant tea and combine it with the apple base.
3. Take a hydrometer potential-alcohol reading; the first recorded reading was 10%.
4. Increase fermentable sugar by an unrecorded amount; the subsequent reading was approximately 11% potential alcohol.
5. Ferment the approximately 2 L must in PET away from direct sunlight.
6. Primary fermentation completed; the batch was confirmed to be in active secondary fermentation by 2026-09-04.
7. During secondary, the wine remained plenty bubbly. Some fermentation foam dried on the inside of the PET vessel.
8. Planned next action: rack carefully into a clean PET vessel, add pectic enzyme at the manufacturer's dose, minimize splashing and headspace, and return the wine to an airlock so fermentation can continue.
9. Take a hydrometer reading after the rack and repeat it after roughly 3 days. Do not move to clearing until gravity is unchanged across repeated readings.
10. Once fermentation is stable, allow the wine to clarify for roughly 1–3+ weeks, checking clarity rather than forcing a fixed date.
11. Rack once more off the clarified sediment.
12. Confirm the wine remains stable before bottling. Bottle as still wine with no priming sugar, then age in bottle.

## Added Plan — 2026-09-05

The brewer's current plan is to rack because the batch is still actively fermenting and has dried fermentation foam on the PET bottle, add pectic enzyme to help with the apple pectin haze, allow secondary fermentation to continue under airlock, then wait for a stable hydrometer reading before treating the batch as finished. Once fermentation is complete, the wine should be left to clarify, racked one final time off the settled sediment, checked again for stability, then bottled as a still wine and aged.

The key adjustment to the original idea is that clarity is not used as proof that fermentation is complete. Gravity stability comes first; clarification and bottling follow.

## Quality Plan

Keep oxygen exposure low during racking, keep the vessel away from direct sunlight, avoid cold-crashing while fermentation is clearly active, and avoid bottling while there is still measurable fermentation. The apple, black tea, mint, plum, and cinnamon flavours should be allowed time to integrate during maturation.
