## Process

1. Sanitise the fermenter, tools, juicer, and anything that will contact the must.
2. Juice the apples and record the juice volume and gravity before adding honey.
3. Decide the target strength from the measured juice gravity before calculating the initial honey addition.
4. Add only the initial portion of honey needed for the chosen starting gravity; keep the remaining honey in reserve.
5. Pitch the high-alcohol-tolerance wine yeast. If a nutrient schedule is used, record the product, amounts, and timing.
6. Monitor fermentation activity and gravity. Add reserved honey only if fermentation is healthy and further alcohol/sweetness is still desired.
7. Do not assume the entire 3.7 kg honey supply must be used.
8. Rack from primary once fermentation is sufficiently complete/stable and the batch is ready to come off the gross lees.
9. Taste before making secondary additions. Cranberry juice and/or spices may be added in measured amounts if desired, with each actual addition logged.
10. Allow the cyser to clear and condition before deciding the final bottling method and aging period.

## Notes

- Prefer pressed/juiced apples over filling the fermenter with grated fruit pulp. If any solids are used later, record their amount and manage the fruit cap during active fermentation.
- Pectic enzyme may be considered for fresh apple juice if available; record the exact product and label-directed dose if used.
- The recipe deliberately leaves volume, gravity, ABV, nutrient dose, spice additions, and final honey usage unset until they are actually measured or chosen.
