## Additions

### Fruit

Cranberry juice and diced sweet plums. For a repeat, wash sound fruit, remove stones, weigh the flesh and record juice volume. Stone removal was not explicitly reported for this batch.

### Sugar Sources

Cranberry juice, plums, maple syrup and the instant tea contribute sugar. An additional 50 g light brown sugar was added at the 2026-09-22 secondary rack. The liquid reading may not include all sugars subsequently released from the plum pieces. Do not convert the approximate potential reading into an invented original SG or claim a precise final ABV.

### Tea / Tannins

Whittard's UK listing for Sour Cherry Instant Tea (358564) identifies sugar, citric acid, beetroot juice powder containing maltodextrin, black-tea extract and natural flavouring. It lists **94 g sugars per 100 g powder** and no cherry fruit ingredient. Treat this as a sweetened flavour addition, not a dose of ordinary black tea leaves.

Source checked 7 September 2026: [Whittard product and nutrition](https://www.whittard.co.uk/tea/tea-type/instant-tea/sour-cherry-instant-tea-358564.html). The brewer's packaging takes precedence if its formulation differs.

### Herbs / Spices

Two cinnamon sticks added, reported in the follow-up. Check intensity at the next progress check and remove once sufficient; remove with the plum solids if still present. No additional spice is planned.

### Acids

No separate acid addition reported. Cranberry, plums and the tea's citric acid already contribute acidity. No automatic acid addition is planned; assess the finished wine before adjustment.

## Bottling

- Default plan: dry, still wine; **no priming sugar**.
- Bottle only after fermentation has finished, repeated gravity readings are unchanged over at least three days, and no restart or unexplained residual sweetness suggests a stall.
- Stable gravity alone can also indicate a stalled fermentation. If unexpectedly sweet/high, investigate before sealing.
- Use cleaned, sanitised wine bottles and suitable closures; bottle sizes and yield are unrecorded.
- Rack gently without splashing or carrying over sediment. Record final gravity, actual packaged volume, number of bottles, date and any losses.
- No backsweetening is assumed. If sweetness is wanted, trial it in a serving glass first. Adding maple, juice or tea powder to the bulk wine can restart fermentation and needs a separate stabilisation plan.
- Store cool and dark; schedule a first tasting about one month after actual bottling.

## Process

### Reported setup — working date 7 September 2026

1. Thoroughly scrubbed/cleaned the vessel and sanitised using the established no-rinse approach; sanitised airlock fitted. Product, dilution and contact time were not supplied.
2. Combined cranberry juice, approximately five diced large sweet plums and Whittard sour cherry tea.
3. Added a generous dollop of maple syrup, reaching approximately 9% on the hydrometer's potential-alcohol scale.
4. Added a little pectic enzyme, a proportionate amount of nutrient and the usual wine yeast.

### Follow-up addition

The brewer also reports adding two cinnamon sticks “for good luck”. The exact addition time is unrecorded; this supplements the original setup report.

### Secondary rack — 22 September 2026

1. Racked the batch from primary into secondary fermentation.
2. Added 50 g light brown sugar.
3. Added additional yeast, yeast nutrient/food and pectic enzyme. The latter three were dosed appropriately for the batch volume; exact quantities were not recorded.
4. No tasting was performed at the rack.
5. Whether the plum solids and cinnamon sticks were retained or removed was not reported and is left explicitly unknown.

### Proposed next steps

1. Continue secondary fermentation under airlock and monitor activity, sediment and clarification.
2. Take a gravity reading at the next useful checkpoint.
3. If gravity is still falling, continue secondary fermentation rather than forcing a clearing or bottling date.
4. Once fermentation is stable, assess whether another rack is useful based on sediment.
5. Bottle only when the completion and stability conditions above are met.

The daily fruit-cap approach is supported by the [American Homebrewers Association's fruit-mead guidance](https://homebrewersassociation.org/beyond-beer/hydromel-how-to-make-session-strength-mead/); its use here is an adaptation to this fruit-wine experiment.
