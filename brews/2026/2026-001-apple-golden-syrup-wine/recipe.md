## Process

1. Ferment the apple wine base with yeast and rapadura.
2. Record the hydrometer potential-alcohol scale reading of 11% taken during the batch.
3. Brew 1 packet of Lidl Very Berry tea with 3 heaped spoonfuls of sugar; cool before mixing into the wine.
4. Continue fermentation in PET away from direct sunlight.
5. Rack the fermented wine off the sediment into a clean PET vessel.
6. Dissolve 90 g pure maple syrup in 160 mL hot water and add it to the racked wine for secondary fermentation.
7. Keep the secondary vessel under airlock in a dark closet.
8. Take a tasting sample during secondary; the 2026-09-04 sample was clear but not fully brilliant, crisp, wine-like, alcohol-forward, and relatively neutral aromatically.
9. Skip the immediate hydrometer check and instead rack to a clean PET bottle, add pectic enzyme according to the product label, refit the airlock, and allow the wine to clarify.
10. Check clarity and sediment formation after several days.
11. Take a hydrometer reading before any sweetening or final bottling.
12. Rack off the clearing sediment once sufficient clarification has occurred.
13. Decide whether to trial orange zest for aroma; use only colored zest and remove it once the desired aroma is reached.
14. Bench-test the finished-wine sweetener on a small sample before deciding whether to backsweeten the full batch.
15. Do not use sodium benzoate as a clearing agent.
16. Bottle only after the wine is clear enough for the intended style and gravity is stable.

## Notes

The batch display name was updated to **Rapadura Very Berry** on 2026-09-05. The existing folder name and batch ID remain unchanged in accordance with repository rules.

The original 2026-08-31 repository entry referred to golden syrup. Later brewer notes identify rapadura as the pre-secondary sugar source and document the Very Berry tea and maple-syrup secondary step. Historical log entries are preserved and corrections are recorded separately.

On 2026-09-04 the wine was sampled during secondary: clear but not fully brilliant, crisp, distinctly wine-like, alcohol-forward, with satisfactory tannin but limited aromatic character. Orange zest is being considered as a finishing aromatic element after clarification.

Final ABV has not yet been measured and should not be inferred from the potential-alcohol scale alone.
