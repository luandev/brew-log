## Process

1. Prepare the apple wine base in PET with apple juice and golden syrup. The original conversation reports approximately 1.2 L at this point; exact golden-syrup quantity was not recorded.
2. Prepare approximately 1 L of tea using black tea, mint, and Whittard Plum & Cinnamon instant tea / sugar mix. The exact tea, mint, and instant-powder quantities were not recorded.
3. Take a hydrometer potential-alcohol reading. The first recorded reading was 10%.
4. Increase the fermentable sugar concentration by an unrecorded amount; the next hydrometer image was read at approximately 11% potential alcohol.
5. Ferment in PET away from direct sunlight. Indirect room light was discussed as acceptable; keeping the vessel dark / shaded was preferred.
6. Later batch notes record rapadura and a Lidl Very Berry tea addition brewed with 3 heaped spoonfuls of sugar. These later notes are preserved alongside the original golden-syrup / plum-cinnamon conversation because exact sequencing is not fully measured.
7. Once the first fermentation is complete, rack the wine off the sediment into a clean PET vessel.
8. Dissolve 90 g pure maple syrup in 160 mL hot water, then add it to the racked wine for secondary fermentation.
9. Keep the secondary vessel vented / under airlock until fermentation is complete and gravity is stable.
10. Planned quality-finishing path discussed for the batch: rack off sediment, cold crash / clear after fermentation is stable, transfer to sanitized glass with minimal headspace, and allow time for maturation before final bottling.

## Quality Plan Discussed

The conversation included a refinement plan for producing a cleaner still wine: ferment to a stable final hydrometer reading, rack carefully without splashing, allow several weeks for clearing, cold crash if useful, and age before bottling. Longer aging was discussed as beneficial for the apple component. These are process plans / recommendations, not completed actions unless separately logged.
